A CALM ORDER OF OPERATIONS

Start with survival. Build toward recovery.

This page helps you decide what to address first. It is an index—not a substitute for the detailed, source-specific instructions in the library.

If someone is in immediate danger

Contact emergency services if any are functioning. Move away from fire, smoke, unstable structures, downed wires, floodwater, violence, leaking fuel, and unknown chemical or radiation hazards. Do not enter a dangerous area to retrieve equipment.

STAGE 1

The first hour: stop conditions from getting worse

  1. Check the scene before acting. Identify active hazards, a safe meeting point, and an escape route.
  2. Account for people. Locate household or group members and identify who needs urgent help.
  3. Address life threats within your training. Use a reputable first-aid reference; do not improvise invasive treatment.
  4. Prevent fire and poisoning. Keep fuel-burning devices outside and away from doors, windows, and vents. Treat downed wires as energized.
  5. Protect critical supplies. Move clean water, medicines, food, documents, radios, lights, and weather protection to the safest accessible location.

STAGE 2

The first 24 hours: stabilize essentials

Safe water

Inventory water before rationing it, separate water of uncertain quality, and choose the safest available source. Follow an authoritative treatment guide for the specific contamination risk; clear-looking water is not automatically safe.

Weather-safe shelter

Choose a location away from continuing hazards. Prioritize staying dry, controlling heat loss or heat exposure, ventilation, safe exits, and separation of flames or exhaust from living areas.

Health and sanitation

Set up handwashing and a waste plan early. Record injuries, symptoms, allergies, regular medicines, treatment given, and changes over time.

Food and information

Inventory food and its condition. Use unsafe-to-store perishables only when they remain safe to eat, and protect shelf-stable food from heat, moisture, pests, and contamination. Keep a written situation log rather than relying on memory.

STAGE 3

The first week: create dependable systems

  • Water: estimate daily demand, establish collection and treatment workflows, assign clean containers, and plan wastewater disposal.
  • Food: make a written inventory and menu, track calories and special dietary needs, then plan replenishment before supplies become critical.
  • Shelter: inspect damage repeatedly, improve drainage and weatherproofing, and create safe cooking, heating, sleeping, and storage areas.
  • Energy: list essential loads first, measure consumption where possible, protect batteries, and separate generation equipment from living spaces.
  • Health: schedule hygiene, monitor wounds and illness, isolate contagious symptoms when appropriate, and maintain medical records.
  • Organization: assign roles, use checklists, hold short daily briefings, and keep a visible list of decisions, supplies, risks, and unfinished work.

STAGE 4

Weeks and months: move from consumption to production

Grow food before stored food runs out

Start with climate, season, soil, water, seed, labor, and time-to-harvest—not with preservation. Build a crop calendar, prioritize reliable calorie and nutrient sources, start soil improvement, and plan storage and seed saving alongside planting.

Build maintainable infrastructure

Document loads and resources before choosing power equipment. Favor systems the group can inspect, repair, and fuel. Establish workshops, spare-parts storage, maintenance schedules, and safety rules.

Restore community functions

Create transparent ways to make decisions, settle disputes, share work, protect vulnerable people, teach skills, maintain health and sanitation, preserve records, communicate with neighbors, and exchange goods fairly.

Keep learning and checking assumptions

Conditions change. Review plans after failures, near misses, illness, weather events, and harvests. Preserve what worked in written procedures so knowledge is not held by only one person.

Keep high-risk work within the limits of your knowledge and equipment.

Medical procedures, structural work, mains electricity, pressurized systems, hazardous chemicals, radiation, and damaged fuel systems can kill without obvious warning. Prefer authoritative sources, verify instructions, use protective equipment, and seek trained help whenever it is available.
